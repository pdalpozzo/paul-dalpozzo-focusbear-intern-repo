## Research & Learn
### What are the risks of using a laptop without an external monitor or keyboard?

Laptops are designed for short term and mobile use. They do not fit into a 'work safe' device for long periods, such as a work day.  

- **Display** - the laptop display is low - it can cause neck, shoulders and upper back issues
- **Keyboard** - the keyboard is made to fit into a small area - it can cause wrist, arm, and finger issues
- **Chair** - being a mobile device, people often overlook the importance of their sitting position - it can cause lower back, elbow, and leg issues

### What ergonomic equipment can improve posture when working on a laptop? (e.g., laptop stands, external keyboards, ergonomic chairs)
- **Laptop Stands** - this stand will elevate the laptop (and its screen) to an approapriate eye level
- **External Monitor** - this is an alternate solution to keeping your main screen at eye level
- **External Keyboard & Mouse** - using an external keyboard will provide better placement of your wrists and reduce strain on arms, wrists and fingers
- **Ergonomic Chair** - a good ergonomic chair will help your posture and reduce strain on your back. It will also help your elbows to be in a good position
- **Vertical Mouse** - better than a normal mouse. It helps reduce the strain on your wrist by keeping it in a more natural state
- **Split Keyboard** - best keyboard to use. It allows you to place each half of the keyboard in line with your elbow, reducing the sideways strain your forearms and wrists would get from keeping your hands close together on a standard keyboard.

### What adjustments should be made to monitor height, chair position, and desk setup for a healthier workspace?
I got this from Google's AI. I mostly agree with it.

|Item | Ergonomic Target | Simple Test/Solution |
|---|---|---|
| Monitor Height | Place at arms length in front of you. Your eye level should be in the top third of the screen. | Place the monitor on books or boxes if it is not adjustable. |
|Chair Position | Hips and knees should be at 90 degrees. Thighs parallel to the floor and feet completely flat. | Adjust the chair height first. If your feet dangle, get a foot stool for them to rest flat. |
|Desk Setup | Desk height should align so that your forearms rest parallel to the floor. Elbows should stay between 90 to 100 degrees. | If your elbow is below your desk, raise your chair. |
| Keyboard & Mouse| Place them close to the edge of the desk so that you are not reaching for them. | Your upper arm should be vertical and you should not need to move your elbow forward to reach the devices. |
