## Why do you think Focus Bear was created?
Jeremy was looking for an app to help him focus on tasks and give him the nudges needed to keep on track. So he created Focus Bear. He was diagonsed with ADHD; being distracted and focusing on the wrong things can happen easily because the mind wants to wonder or thinks something else is more important at that time.
The following is directly from the website.  

>There are several reasons behind these performance challenges:
>
>Distractions from social media, entertainment sites and messaging services make it harder than ever to stay focused.
>Difficulties in detaching from hyper-focused states cause poor punctuality, poor deadline adherence and many other physical and mental health issues that impact productivity such as muscular pain, difficulties detecting interoceptive signals (ignoring hunger, thirst and even the aching necessity to go to the restroom) and overall nutritional balance (many neurodivergent individuals have nutrient deficiencies due to extreme problems with meal prep and grocery shopping – which extend to managing the expiry dates of the groceries stored in their fridge, forgetting storing items in the fridge, forgetting cooked meals, etc.).

>Lack of metacognitive strategies lead to inadequate planning and scheduling.
>Chaotic lives mean that people don’t follow regular routines even though routines including aerobic exercise, yoga and meditation have been shown to help reduce the impact of ADHD symptoms.

This pretty much sums up why the app was created.

## What problem is Focus Bear solving?
It is trying to solve the problem of ADHD affecting the amount and quality of production done by a person. People with ADHD have trouble focusing and this results in them being less effective. Doesn't matter if the task is work related or a hobby.  
'Having trouble focusing' sounds like they can't keep a thought in their head long enough to follow. But it is more like having too many thoughts. This overloads the mind and makes it hard to even create a mental priority list. One of the blogs call this 'choice paralysis'. The app helps the user track it all but also tries to avoid letting the users current task consume them.   

Blocking distractions also helps with this. Removing that stray thought of 'what has happened on <insert social media> today?' A lot of people could benefit from something like that. But Focus Bear is there to check in and help you answer the question 'is that important right now? Could you be spending that time on the task you set? Maybe you need a break?'  
ADHD can also create a focus so intense that things like eating and drinking never enter the train of thought. This compounds as it creates bad habits. Missing a meal is bad, but then it might cause overeating later, which is also bad. Then this creates a cycle that you start accepting and can cause issues with health, which make issues with the mind even worse. Things can spiral out of control very quickly.  

Focus Bear is trying to help the user take back control over their lives by creating routines and habits that are backed by science. While the app is focused on providing this structure and control to people with ADHD, I believe it can be used for many things by many people.   
People with weight control issues (like myself) create these bad habits and put their diet as a 'whatever' choice. Whatever is easiest. Focus Bear could help them to control eating habits by introducing strict eating times, snack times, tracking food eaten, planning exercise, and even setting time aside to prepare meals. Although, I think this starts to be less of a helper for ADHD and starts to become more general.
